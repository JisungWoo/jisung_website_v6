Inside the Snowflake redesign+

Diagnose before rewriting

A simple SELECT against a design view took 2–3 hours to complete. I used Snowflake Query History to investigate slow execution, then worked through nested CTEs, subqueries and dozens of joins to narrow down the expensive parts.

Change the SQL and the processing structure

I reorganized CTEs and WHERE conditions to filter earlier where appropriate, selected specific columns instead of SELECT *, and used suitable data models or reporting views in place of design-view inputs. I also initiated the investigation into additional views and staging tables; the team restructured the pipeline to store intermediate results.

Make the tradeoff explicit

The revised flow adds stored results between transformation layers. That creates a reuse boundary, but also adds storage and load dependencies. I worked with the data architect to agree on the refresh order and frequency. Recovery after a failed load remains a design consideration, not a documented implementation claim here. A CTE alone is not a stored intermediate table.

The full data refresh now completes in approximately 30 minutes. I compared before-and-after row counts and used MINUS queries to check differences between results. Shorter refreshes also reduced the wait when validating logic changes. These are self-reported project results: the earlier 2–3 hours measured a SELECT, so the two timings are not a like-for-like benchmark.

Isolate the relationship producing duplicates

For duplicate purchase-order records, I reduced the query to the relevant columns and joins, then disabled groups of joins to isolate the relationship producing duplicates. This made a large query small enough for the team to investigate together.

TECHNICAL DELIVERY LEADERSHIP

From business intent
to work the team can implement.

I clarify the work with stakeholders, partner with the data architect on model design, and take responsibility for investigations the offshore team cannot complete without QA or production-data access.

  1. Agree on the requirement and calculation

    I communicate with business stakeholders, directors and senior directors to clarify requirements and calculation logic. I keep the conversation open to validate decisions, confirm scope and incorporate new requirements.

  2. Give engineers an implementation-ready handoff

    I turn the agreed requirements into step-by-step descriptions, supported by visuals, Excel examples, screenshots and code. Engineers receive the context and implementation detail together rather than having to reconstruct the business request.

  3. Own the investigation when access becomes a blocker

    When offshore colleagues cannot inspect QA or production data, I investigate duplicate records, complex query behavior and performance issues, and make changes when needed. I coordinate with stakeholders as the investigation reveals questions or changes to the requirement.
